# Mission Still Not Accomplished in Iraq: Why the United States Should Not Leave
**Author:** Emma Sky | **Week:** 13 | **Type:** RR
**Case Study:** Iraq
**Pages:** 9–15, 15A (*Foreign Affairs*, Vol. 96, No. 6, Nov/Dec 2017)

## Summary

Sky's central argument is that the July 2017 military liberation of Mosul from ISIS must not tempt the Trump administration into premature disengagement. ISIS is a **symptom** of failed governance, not its cause — and without sustained U.S. presence, the conditions that generated ISIS will regenerate.

**What went wrong (the long causation chain):** The 2003 CPA decisions — dismissing Iraqi civil servants and dissolving the security forces — collapsed the state, triggered civil war, gave al-Qaeda in Iraq a foothold, and opened the door to Iranian expansion. Bush's 2007 surge plus the Sunni Awakening reversed this trajectory. But the Obama administration then disengaged at the critical juncture: the 2010 elections. When Iraqiya (Allawi's nonsectarian coalition) narrowly beat Maliki, Obama backed Maliki anyway — playing directly into Iran's hands, since Tehran pressured Sadr to align with Maliki in exchange for guaranteeing the full U.S. withdrawal by 2011. Secure in his second term, Maliki reneged on Sunni Awakening promises, labeled Sunni politicians "terrorists," politicized the military, and created the grievance ecosystem in which ISIS presented itself as Sunni defender. By 2014 ISIS controlled a third of Iraq and the U.S.-trained army had dissolved.

**The 2017 political landscape:** PM Abadi (Maliki's replacement) is more inclusive and seeks to balance Iranian and American influence, but faces pressure from Maliki (still working to undermine him), Iranian-backed militias (Popular Mobilization Units / PMUs seeking to convert military gains into political power), and a fractured Sunni bloc. The Kurdish independence referendum of September 2017 added further volatility. Iran is now the dominant external power in both Iraq and Syria.

**Sky's prescriptions:** (1) Continue and expand support for the Counter Terrorism Service — the most successful U.S. initiative since 2003, cross-sectarian and battle-proven. (2) Security sector reform: integrate state-loyal militias into the Iraqi Security Forces, disarm Iranian proxies. (3) Develop a clear Kurdish policy via negotiated Baghdad–Erbil process with international mediation on Kirkuk. (4) Leverage Gulf states (Saudi Arabia) to invest in Mosul reconstruction — counterbalancing Iranian influence and offering Sunnis a stake in the future. None of this requires U.S. combat forces; it can operate under the existing Strategic Framework Agreement.

Sky's authority is personal: she worked for the CPA, served as Kirkuk's governorate coordinator, and was political adviser to General Odierno — making this simultaneously a policy argument and a participant's retrospective. Relates to: HOW does the occupation end (Sky argues it should not yet), and WHAT the Americans did (and failed to do) at each juncture.

## Key Terms

- **Coalition Provisional Authority (CPA):** The U.S.-led transitional administration governing Iraq 2003–2004; its de-Baathification and military dissolution orders are treated by Sky as the original sin of the occupation
- **De-Baathification:** CPA Order 1 (2003) — dismissal of Baath Party members from government; eliminated experienced bureaucratic capacity and alienated the Sunni administrative class
- **Dissolution of Security Forces:** CPA Order 2 — disbanded the Iraqi military, creating ~400,000 suddenly unemployed, armed men; many later joined insurgent groups
- **Sunni Awakening (Sahwa):** 2006–2007 movement of Sunni tribal leaders who allied with US forces against al-Qaeda in Iraq in exchange for political incorporation; Maliki's betrayal of these promises is the proximate cause of ISIS's rise
- **The Surge (2007):** Bush's escalation of ~30,000 additional US troops; combined with the Sunni Awakening, dramatically reduced sectarian violence — cited as proof that US engagement can reverse negative trajectories
- **Popular Mobilization Units (PMUs / Hashd al-Shaabi):** Iraqi Shiite militias formed in response to Sistani's 2014 fatwa; Iran-backed; militarily effective against ISIS but politically corrosive to state legitimacy and source of Sunni fear
- **Counter Terrorism Service (CTS):** Elite, cross-sectarian US-trained Iraqi force; bore the brunt of Mosul fighting; Sky's model for what successful institution-building looks like
- **Status-of-Forces Agreement (SOFA):** 2008 agreement mandating full US withdrawal by end of 2011; Maliki's deal with Iran to guarantee this withdrawal was the structural mechanism by which Iran leveraged the 2010 election outcome
- **Strategic Framework Agreement:** Separate US-Iraq agreement on long-term cooperation; Sky argues it provides sufficient legal basis for continued US advisory presence without requiring combat forces or new negotiations
- **Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG) / Barzani / Kirkuk:** Semi-autonomous Kurdish region; September 2017 independence referendum (opposed by US, Iran, Turkey); Kirkuk — multiethnic, oil-rich, disputed — is the central contested territory
- **Iraqiya / Allawi:** Nonsectarian nationalist coalition that won 2010 elections; Obama's refusal to recognize its mandate as a governing right was Sky's pivotal turning point
- **Inflection point:** Sky's recurring term for moments when US policy choices determined Iraq's trajectory — 2007 (surge), 2010 (election), 2014 (ISIS), 2017 (post-Mosul)
