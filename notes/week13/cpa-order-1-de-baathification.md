# Coalition Provisional Authority Order No. 1: De-Baathification of Iraqi Society
**Author:** L. Paul Bremer, Administrator, Coalition Provisional Authority | **Week:** 13 | **Type:** PS
**Case Study:** Iraq
**Pages:** 2 pp. (CPA/ORD/16 May 2003/01)

## Summary

Signed by CPA Administrator L. Paul Bremer on May 16, 2003, this two-page order formally implements the April 16 disestablishment of the Iraqi Baath Party. It is the Iraq occupation's functional equivalent of denazification — a sweeping purge of the ruling party from every corner of the Iraqi state. Six provisions define its scope: (1) the Baath Party's structures are dismantled and its leadership removed from all authority; (2) the four ranks of "Senior Party Members" (Regional Command, Branch, Section, and Group Members) are immediately fired, permanently banned from public sector employment, and subjected to criminal investigation and possible detention; (3) the top three management layers in every ministry, corporation, university, and hospital are screened for Baath membership, with any confirmed full members — including junior ranks ('Udw/Member; 'Udw 'Amil/Active Member) — removed; (4) displays of Saddam Hussein's image or Baath symbols in public or government spaces are banned; (5) bounties are offered for information on senior members; (6) Bremer retains personal authority to grant exceptions.

The analytical weight of this document lies in its recklessness of scale. The Baath Party had roughly one million members, the majority of whom joined for career necessity rather than ideological conviction — teachers, doctors, engineers, military officers could not advance without a party card. Removing the top three layers of every ministry and institution in a single order effectively hollowed out Iraqi state capacity overnight, with no reconstruction mechanism, no replacement pipeline, and no timeline. The stated rationale blends three logics — humanitarian (Iraqis "suffered large scale human rights abuses"), security (threat to Coalition forces), and democratic ("representative government in Iraq is not threatened by Baathist elements") — but the order addresses none of the practical consequences of mass displacement. This is occupation policy as political signal rather than administrative planning.

The order directly echoes and then exceeds JCS 1067 in Germany (Week 5), where Allied planners also removed Nazi party members from public employment — but were forced to walk back the purge when it became clear Germany needed its trained administrators to function. The CPA drew the opposite lesson, or none at all. The absence of Iraqi voices in drafting this policy (Bremer signed unilaterally under his self-declared authority) also connects to the self-determination theme: occupied peoples are objects of transformation, not participants in it.

- Central argument: De-Baathification is a prerequisite for legitimate representative government and Coalition security in Iraq.
- Key evidence: Hierarchical rank system used to define purge scope; exception clause preserves unilateral CPA control.
- Connection to course themes: Occupation policy & planning (punitive vs. reformist); democratic reform; self-determination vs. imperial control; collaboration & resistance.
- Guiding questions: WHAT did Americans do? (comprehensive state restructuring); HOW did occupation go wrong? (this order is a primary cause of insurgency by alienating Sunni military/administrative class)

## Key Terms

- **Coalition Provisional Authority (CPA):** The US-led transitional governing body that administered Iraq from April 2003 until June 2004, when it transferred formal sovereignty to the Iraqi Interim Government. Wielded executive, legislative, and judicial authority by fiat.
- **De-Baathification:** The policy of removing members of the Iraqi Baath Party from government, military, and public institutions. Modeled conceptually on denazification in post-WWII Germany but applied more sweepingly and with less institutional planning.
- **L. Paul Bremer:** CPA Administrator from May 2003; issued this order days after his arrival. His unilateral authority to govern by order — with no Iraqi counterpart approval required — encapsulates the imperial structure of CPA rule.
- **Senior Party Members ('Udw Qutriyya, 'Udw Far', 'Udw Shu'bah, 'Udw Firqah):** The four highest party ranks targeted for automatic removal and public-sector ban. The Arabic rank titles reveal the order's awareness of Baath internal structure.
- **"Top three layers of management":** The operative scope mechanism: not just party hierarchy but institutional position. A university president, hospital director, or ministry deputy need not be a senior party member — merely a full member in a senior role — to be purged.
- **Denazification (comparison):** Allied policy in post-WWII Germany to remove Nazi party members from public life (cf. JCS 1067, Week 5). The key difference: US planners in Germany eventually pragmatically rolled back purges; in Iraq, the CPA did not.
- **Sunni insurgency:** The political consequence this order helped produce: Sunni military officers and administrators — the backbone of the Baath state — were simultaneously disarmed (by the companion Order 2 disbanding the army) and economically dispossessed, creating a large pool of armed, aggrieved men with organizational skill and nothing to lose.
