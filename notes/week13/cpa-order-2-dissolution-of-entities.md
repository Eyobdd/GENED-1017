# CPA Order No. 2: Dissolution of Entities (2003)
**Author:** L. Paul Bremer, CPA Administrator | **Week:** 13 | **Type:** PS
**Case Study:** Iraq
**Pages:** CPA/ORD/23 May 2003/02 (5 pp. + Annex)

## Summary
Issued May 23, 2003, CPA Order No. 2 formally dissolved all major Iraqi military, intelligence, and security institutions. Signed by L. Paul Bremer seven days after Order No. 1 (De-Baathification), it dissolved roughly 23 named entities — the entire Iraqi Army, Air Force, Navy, Republican Guard, intelligence services, paramilitaries, and key government organs — and dismissed all their employees effective April 16, 2003. Soldiers of Colonel rank or above were defined as "Senior Party Members" and barred from any termination payment or pension. Conscription was suspended indefinitely. A promised replacement, the "New Iraqi Corps," was sketched in a single paragraph with no timeline.

The document's analytical significance lies in what it *doesn't* say: it provides no plan for absorbing ~400,000 suddenly unemployed, armed, and humiliated men into civilian life. The preamble frames dissolution as liberation ("the prior Iraqi regime used certain government entities to oppress the Iraqi people"), echoing the rhetorical logic of denazification — that erasing the old state apparatus *is* reform. But where WWII occupiers in Japan and Germany retained significant bureaucratic capacity while purging leadership, CPA Order No. 2 went all the way down. The result fueled the Sunni insurgency that would define the occupation's trajectory. This order — alongside Order No. 1 — represents the "tabula rasa" theory of state-building at its most radical: the assumption that destruction of the old state is the precondition for a new one.

Guiding questions: **WHAT did they do?** (total dissolution of Iraqi security apparatus) / **HOW did the occupation end?** (this decision directly precipitated insurgency and a failed occupation).

## Key Terms
- **Coalition Provisional Authority (CPA)**: The transitional governing body for Iraq, 2003–2004, headed by L. Paul Bremer under appointment by the US Secretary of Defense. Operated outside normal military chain of command; had no democratic accountability to Iraqis.
- **De-Baathification**: The companion policy (CPA Order No. 1) removing Ba'ath Party members from public life, analogous to denazification. Order No. 2 is its military dimension.
- **Senior Party Member**: Defined in Order No. 2 as any person holding rank of Colonel or above — a functional equation of military seniority with political complicity, excluding the entire officer corps from benefits.
- **Dissolved Entities**: The ~23 institutions named in the Annex, including the entire Iraqi military, intelligence services, paramilitaries, and key regime organs (Republican Guard, Presidential Diwan, Revolutionary Command Council, etc.).
- **New Iraqi Corps**: The promised replacement force, mentioned only in §5 with no implementation details — widely seen as an afterthought.
- **Tabula rasa state-building**: The theory, implicit in Orders 1 and 2, that full erasure of existing state institutions is the precondition for democratic reconstruction — the opposite of the WWII approach in Japan and Germany, which retained significant bureaucratic infrastructure.
- **L. Paul Bremer**: CPA Administrator, former diplomat, appointed May 2003. Signed Orders 1 and 2 within his first two weeks in office.
