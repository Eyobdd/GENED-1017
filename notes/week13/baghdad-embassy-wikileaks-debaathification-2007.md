**Author:** Ambassador Ryan Crocker (US Embassy Baghdad) | **Week:** 13 | **Type:** PS
**Case Study:** Iraq
**Pages:** N/A — diplomatic cable (2 pp.)
**Source:** WikiLeaks Canonical ID: 07BAGHDAD3728_a | Date: November 13, 2007

## Summary

This classified diplomatic cable — marked SENSITIVE, UNCLASSIFIED/FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY — reports on interviews conducted by Provincial Reconstruction Team (PRT) officers with four senior Diyala Province executives (2 Sunni, 2 Shia; 3 elected, 1 appointed). All four, speaking anonymously, gave near-identical assessments: CPA Order Number 1 (May 2003), which purged all Ba'athists from public life and government employment, was a catastrophic mistake that continues to cripple Iraqi governance four years on.

Their core argument: Ba'ath party membership under Saddam was compulsory for career advancement — not evidence of ideological loyalty. The original pan-Arab, secular Ba'athist ideology had nothing to do with Saddam's party, which was purely an instrument of personal power. By stripping the top three layers of every ministry and technical office, CPA Order #1 left the least qualified people in charge. Officials described it as "decapitating the nation."

All four advocated a conditional rehabilitation: screen and vet former Ba'athists; those with "no bloody hands" (no involvement in criminal or violent acts) should be permitted to return to public life — not to power, but as skilled administrators and facilitators. Continued exclusion, they argued, only drives Ba'athists underground, pushing them into sub-rosa political activity from exile.

Ambassador Crocker's closing comment highlights the remarkable cross-sectarian consensus: Sunni and Shia officials who personally benefited from de-Ba'athification nonetheless speak with one voice that the policy has failed.

**Relates to guiding questions:**
- **WHAT did they do?** CPA imposed sweeping de-Ba'athification, modeled partly on de-Nazification, that destroyed institutional capacity.
- **HOW did occupation end? / What were its results?** By 2007 the policy is widely acknowledged as a failure even by those it empowered — reconciliation stalled, governance dysfunctional, insurgency fueled.

## Key Terms

- **CPA Order Number 1 (De-Ba'athification of Iraqi Society, May 2003):** Coalition Provisional Authority decree issued under L. Paul Bremer that removed all Iraqis with Ba'ath party membership from public life and government employment. Purged the top four levels of Ba'ath party members across all ministries. Widely compared to — but far more sweeping than — de-Nazification in occupied Germany.

- **De-Ba'athification:** The process of removing former Ba'ath party members from Iraqi institutions. Cf. de-Nazification (Germany, JCS 1067) — same logic, different context and outcome. In Germany, a partial "reverse course" rehabilitated former Nazis into West German state-building; in Iraq, no equivalent reversal occurred.

- **Provincial Reconstruction Team (PRT):** Interagency US teams embedded in Iraqi provinces combining civilian and military personnel, tasked with governance capacity-building. The source of this cable's reporting.

- **"Bloody hands" standard:** Informal criterion proposed by Diyala officials for distinguishing rehabilitable Ba'athists (those who joined for career reasons) from those who committed crimes under Saddam. Contrasts with CPA's blanket exclusion based solely on party rank.

- **Sub-rosa activities:** Covert political activities. Diyala officials' argument: exclusion doesn't eliminate Ba'athist influence, it just drives it underground and across borders — potentially making it more dangerous.

- **Governance Center (GC):** Site where PRT officers conducted the anonymous interviews; a provincial governance hub in Diyala.

- **Sectarian reconciliation:** The broader political goal that de-Ba'athification was meant to serve (removing Sunni-dominated Saddam loyalists) but which officials argue it has instead obstructed, by stripping competent administrators and fueling grievance.
