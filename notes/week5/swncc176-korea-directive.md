## Summary

SWNCC 176/8, issued 13 October 1945 as a Top Secret directive to the Commander in Chief, U.S. Army Forces, Pacific, is the operational framework for the American occupation of Korea south of the 38th parallel. Read alongside SWNCC 150 (Japan) and JCS 1067 (Germany), it completes the triptych of American occupation directives and its internal tensions reveal the fundamental contradictions in US occupation ideology.

**The liberationist frame:** Section 3a states the "ultimate objective" is to "foster conditions which will bring about the establishment of a free and independent nation capable of taking her place as a responsible and peaceful member of the family of nations." Section 3c enumerates four specific goals: ensure Japanese surrender compliance; effect complete political/administrative separation from Japan; facilitate a sound Korean economy; and "foster the establishment of local self-government and the restoration of a free and independent nation which will conform with the principles expressed in the Charter of the United Nations." The Cairo Declaration (1943) is invoked throughout — Korea is to be treated as a *liberated* country, not an enemy (unlike Germany or Japan). The pathway envisioned: initial US-Soviet civil administration → four-power trusteeship (US, UK, China, USSR) → eventual independence.

**The operative contradictions:** Against this liberationist language, Section 9g administers the decisive blow: "You will not extend official recognition to, nor utilize for political purposes, any self-styled Korean provisional government or similar political organizations." This single clause dissolved the Korean People's Republic (KPR), the grassroots government formed in September 1945 under Lyuh Woon-hyung. Section 2b compounds this: while Korea is theoretically "liberated," the commander is "vested with the conventional powers of a military occupant of enemy territory." The Section 5a instruction to "continue the substantive and procedural law existing in Korea at the commencement of your occupation" preserved the colonial legal apparatus even while abrogating its most overtly discriminatory elements.

**The Korean difference from Japan and Germany:** Unlike Japan (SWNCC 150), where MacArthur ruled indirectly through the Emperor, SWNCC 176 establishes direct US Military Government. Unlike Germany (JCS 1067), which was purely punitive, Korea is framed as liberation — but the operative instructions dissolve Korean self-organizing capacity just as thoroughly. Section 5f permits "temporary" use of Japanese personnel and Korean collaborators if technically qualified Koreans are unavailable, which in practice meant the colonial police and bureaucracy survived. As Chang documents, this was how "independence without liberation" was administratively produced: reformist language in the objectives, colonial continuity in the implementation.

**The Cold War dimension:** Section 4d instructs the commander to "establish liaison with the Russians and seek through that liaison to achieve the maximum uniformity of procedures and policies in the control of Korea." The 38th parallel appears in Section 8 only as a displaced-persons management problem — Koreans from the north may return. The directive was written before the full Cold War logic had crystallized, yet its prohibitions (Section 9g) aligned with the US posture of favoring rightist over leftist Korean political actors.

## Key Terms

- **SWNCC 176/8**: State-War-Navy Coordinating Committee document 176, eighth revision, October 13, 1945. Top Secret operational directive for Korea occupation. Assigned pages 4-7 cover Sections 3-6 (objectives, civil affairs structure, political reorganization, demilitarization).
- **Section 9g**: The prohibition on recognizing "any self-styled Korean provisional government or similar political organizations." The operative clause that dissolved the Korean People's Republic — Chang's key analytical target. **Note:** The earlier processed version of this note mistakenly cited this as "Section 5g."
- **Korean People's Republic (KPR)**: Grassroots Korean government formed September 1945 under Lyuh Woon-hyung; had established local People's Committees across Korea within weeks of liberation. Dissolved by US Military Government under authority of §9g. Chang argues this was the critical turning point converting decolonization into "independence without liberation."
- **"Liberated country"**: The document's framing of Korea — contrasts with Germany and Japan (enemy nations). But §2a simultaneously vests the commander with "conventional powers of a military occupant of enemy territory," creating the foundational contradiction.
- **Direct vs. indirect rule**: SWNCC 176 establishes direct US Military Government in Korea; SWNCC 150 used indirect rule through the Emperor in Japan. The difference: Japan's indirect rule directed Japanese agency through legitimate institutions; Korea's direct rule displaced existing Korean agency (KPR, People's Committees).
- **Trusteeship pathway**: §2b envisions: initial US-Soviet civil administration → four-power trusteeship (US, UK, China, USSR) → eventual independence. The trusteeship stage proved the most politically explosive with Koreans, who expected immediate independence.
- **38th parallel**: Appears in §8f-h only as a displaced persons management problem; the directive was written before the full Cold War division logic had hardened.
- **"Foster conditions"**: Governing language for both SWNCC 176 (Korea) and SWNCC 150 (Japan) — the US role as facilitator of democratization. But §9g's prohibition shows what "fostering conditions" actually meant operationally.

## Cross-Reading Connections

- **Chang, "Independence Without Liberation" (W5)**: SWNCC 176 §9g is the primary document Chang analyzes. Chang shows §9g's prohibition was not politically neutral — it dissolved the KPR while implicitly clearing the field for rightist collaborators. Section 3's liberationist language and §9g's operative restriction are the documentary embodiment of Chang's thesis.

- **SWNCC 150 Japan (W5)**: Both use reformist self-determination language. Difference: SWNCC 150 → indirect rule through Emperor; SWNCC 176 → direct Military Government + prohibition on Korean political organizations. The comparison illuminates how the same US government applied fundamentally different governance logics across two simultaneous occupations.

- **JCS 1067 Germany (W5)**: JCS 1067 is explicitly punitive (Germany = defeated enemy). SWNCC 176 is nominally liberationist (Korea = liberated friend). Yet both produced similar operative effects: dissolution of existing political organizations, retention of colonial/Nazi administrative personnel (with exceptions), security apparatus maintained under US control. Different rhetoric, convergent practice.

- **Atlantic Charter / UN Charter (W4)**: SWNCC 176 §3c invokes the UN Charter as the legitimizing framework for Korean independence. But §9g's prohibition on recognizing Korean self-government shows the gap between Atlantic Charter self-determination principles and operational occupation policy — exactly the "contradiction" Duara (W4) analyzes as characteristic of post-war American empire.

- **US/Soviet occupation flyers (W5)**: Soviet flyer: "Korea has become a free state!" US flyer: warned against "hasty and ill-advised acts." SWNCC 176 is the policy backdrop for that US warning — §9g is the operative document authorizing suppression of the KPR and the labor movement the Soviet flyer rhetorically celebrated.

- **Dower, *Embracing Defeat* Ch.6 (W5)**: Dower's "contradictions of democratic revolution from above" in Japan apply directly to Korea. Both occupations imposed democratic reform while maintaining full military authority; both used existing elites selectively. Korea's contradictions were sharper because the US simultaneously faced decolonization from Japan AND Cold War competition with the USSR.
